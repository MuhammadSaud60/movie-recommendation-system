How It Works

The simple science behind the recommendations.

What is a Content-Based Recommendation?

To recommend movies, I use a method called Content-Based Recommendation. This simply means I look at the "content" or details of the movie itself to find matches. If you tell me you love a sci-fi movie directed by James Cameron with aliens and space travel, I will search for other movies that share those exact same details.

How I Built This

I started with a large database of movies called the TMDB 5000 dataset. First, I took all the important details about a movie—like its overview, the genres, the cast, and the director. I combined all these words into one giant paragraph, or "tag," for every single movie.

However, computers do not understand words; they only understand numbers. So, I had to translate those words.

1. Creating Vectors

To turn the words into numbers, I used a Python tool called CountVectorizer (from a library called sklearn). This tool looks at my giant list of movies and counts how many times specific words appear.

By counting the words, the tool turns my paragraph of text into a long list of numbers. In math and programming, a list of numbers that represents something is called a Vector. Now, every single movie in my database is represented by its own unique Vector.

2. Finding the Distance

Now that every movie is just a list of numbers, how do I know which ones are similar? I compare them using a tool called cosine_similarity.

Imagine drawing a straight line on a graph for every movie based on its numbers. Cosine similarity measures the angle between those lines to see how close they are to pointing in the exact same direction.

This tool gives me a simple score between 0 and 1:

  • A score of 1 means the movies are very similar (their lines point the exact same way).
  • A score of 0 means they have absolutely nothing in common.

When you type in a movie, my system quickly checks its Vector against all the other movies, finds the scores that are closest to 1, and shows you the top 5 results!
